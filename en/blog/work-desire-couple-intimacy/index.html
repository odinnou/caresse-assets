Running a business, with work still on your mind

Clients, teams, accounts and unexpected problems can fill the day. In the evening, the task list may keep running. If your partner also has a demanding job, who comes up with an idea, sets the mood and suggests what comes next? Some women entrepreneurs may recognise this, but it does not describe every woman or every couple.

Mental load is a way of describing that experience here, not a diagnosis. Tiredness, health, your relationship and personal circumstances can also shape desire.

Stress and sexuality: what research can tell us

A clinical review published in 2024 describes links between stress and women’s sexual function [1]. Stress can affect sexuality, but it cannot explain every change in desire. The review concerns women and stress broadly, not women entrepreneurs specifically.

Research describes patterns in groups. It cannot predict what you will want tonight or show that an audio tool will remove work stress.

Does sex make you perform better at work?

A two-week daily diary study of married employees found an association between sexual activity the previous evening and positive mood, job satisfaction and engagement the next day [2].

It does not demonstrate higher objective productivity, an effect specific to orgasm or greater entrepreneurial success. This is observational research: association alone cannot establish causation. It does not test Caresse. Pleasure does not need to earn its place by improving work.

Sources and limitations

  1. Evidence for the Impact of Stress and Trauma on Sexual Function in Women (2024). Clinical review of stress and women’s sexual function; not research on Caresse or specifically on entrepreneurship.
  2. From the Bedroom to the Office: Workplace Spillover Effects of Sexual Activity at Home (2017/2019). Observational study, first published online in 2017: self-reported mood, job satisfaction and engagement, without proof of a productivity gain.
